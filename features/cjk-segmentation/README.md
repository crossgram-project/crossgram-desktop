# CJK words in the text of the client

Chinese and Japanese are written without spaces between words, so a text
selection has no separator to cut at and only a dictionary of the language
tells the words apart. The client knows punctuation alone: `IsWordSeparator`
in `ui/text/text.cpp` is a list of ASCII punctuation, and Qt's own cursor
movement uses the same idea in `QTextEngine::atWordSeparator`. One run of such
characters is therefore one word for both of them, which is what the mobile
clients do differently: Android and iOS hand them a dictionary of the language
through their platform break iterator.

The punctuation of those scripts is missing from the list as well, so a
sentence that is written with fullwidth punctuation has no separator in it at
all, and a double click on it selects everything to the end of the message.

## What the patch does

`text_crossgram.inc` adds a dictionary, and
`Ui::Text::WordSegment` in `ui/text/text.h` exposes it:

- `IsSeparator` answers the question `IsWordSeparator` answers, with the
  punctuation of these scripts and the spaces Qt itself moves the cursor over
  added, and an apostrophe that stands inside a word belonging to that word,
  the way the newest upstream reads it;
- `Start` and `End` bounds the word around a position, which are the words Qt
  cannot see;
- `MoveForward` and `MoveBackward` move over the text one word at a time;
- `IsRightToLeft` reports text Qt moves over visually, where the patch keeps
  out of the way.

A run of characters of these scripts is split by the frequency dictionary: for
every position the word that maximizes the sum of the logarithms of the
frequencies of the words a segmentation is made of wins, and a character the
dictionary does not know becomes a word of its own. Every function reads a
string that starts at a multiple of `kChunkLength` in the coordinates of its
caller; only one such chunk is split at a time, which bounds the work for a run
of any length and lets a caller that owns a window of a text - the keyboard
selection of the message history, which has only the symbol it asks for -
pass that window instead of the whole message.

The patch replaces the words branch of `String::adjustSelection`, which is
what every text selection of the client goes through: a message, a caption, a
label, a poll, an article of the editor. It replaces the words branch of the
keyboard selection of the message history, and it adds the same words to
`Ui::InputField`: Ctrl+Left/Right (Option+Left/Right on macOS, the sequences
Qt itself uses) move by words, a double click selects one, and the drag that
follows grows that selection by the words it passes.

## The dictionary

`data/words.bin` holds 114791 words of at most 15 UTF-16 units, as the
version of the format, the count, the longest word, the score of a character
the list does not know, the offsets of the words, the score of each word next
to its offset and the words themselves as UTF-8, compressed with zlib for
`qUncompress`. It is generated by

```bash
node features/cjk-segmentation/tools/build-dictionary.mjs <path to jieba dict.txt>
```

from the word list and the frequencies recorded in `data/SOURCES.md`, keeping
the words that appear often enough to matter in a message, and from
`data/supplement.txt`, which adds the words of messaging, applications and the
internet that a list taken from a news corpus of 2014 does not know -
`微信`, `双击`, `表情包` and their traditional spellings, for example. The
generator refuses a word that is not made of ideographs, and the patcher
checks that the file holds together before it packs it into the generated
source.

## What it does not do

- Japanese kana are split per character, because the dictionary is Chinese;
  Korean already separates its words with spaces and is left alone.
- Traditional Chinese words are only known through `data/supplement.txt`; the
  rest of a traditional text is split per character, which is still better
  than one word for a whole sentence.
- Text that carries right-to-left characters is left to Qt, which moves over it
  visually.
- A word never crosses a chunk boundary, so a word that starts 63 characters
  into a run of more than 64 characters without a separator can be split in
  two. Punctuation and spaces end a run, so this needs a run of that length.

## Tests

`tests/cjk-segmentation.test.ts` patches fixtures that hold both shapes of the
replaced code, checks idempotence, CRLF preservation and the loud failure of a
rewritten anchor, checks the dictionary the patcher ships and the bytes it
packs into the generated source, and compiles the generated word segmentation
with a harness of the Qt types it uses, where the words of a message, a
keyboard step and the patched selection of a double click are exercised.

`tests/cjk-segmentation.e2e.test.ts` runs the same patch against the release
sources of every supported upstream when
`CROSSGRAM_DESKTOP_CJK_SEGMENTATION_SOURCE_ROOT` points at clean snapshots
with one directory per target, and the `check.yml` workflow applies the whole
patch set to every supported release, so a changed upstream fails the check
job instead of losing the words silently.
